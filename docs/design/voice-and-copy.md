# Voice and copy

The owner's stated preference: "I like my language, not the generic front end."
Copy on this site is the owner's voice tightened, never replaced. This file records the
rules that came out of that and a worked example of turning raw talk into beats.

## Rules

1. **Keep the owner's words.** Phrases like "AI Builder", "raise a ticket" and the
   whole Who manifesto came from the owner and stay verbatim, capitals included. Do not upgrade them to marketing.
2. **Headline: one sentence, one emphasised phrase.** Every page `h2` is a plain
   sentence; at most one phrase is italic or red pen:
   "Things I built, then *gave away.*", "Got a messy problem? *Raise a ticket.*"
3. **Labels encode structure.** `p.03 · Built · Open source`, `Exp.01`, `fig. 2`.
   Page, category, then the proper noun. Nothing decorative.
4. **Lead with the outcome, then how.** A beat title is the result in six words;
   the body says what was done in two sentences.
5. **Numbers live in titles.** "Deals closed in 2 days, not 15." "$100M+ in leakage,
   stopped." Body text explains, it does not repeat the number.
6. **Scale is stated, not shouted.** "at Microsoft's scale, billions stopped sitting
   idle for days" carries the point without exclamation marks.
7. **Never promise more than the source says.** The Tazapay beats say "help surface",
   "building AI systems that help", because that is what the owner said. Claims
   about impact come only from the owner's own numbers.
8. **Illustrations are labelled illustrative** and never claim to be product shots.
9. **Talk to the reader in second person only in calls to action.** "Get to know me",
   "Raise a ticket". Elsewhere it is first person or plain description.
10. **Sentence case everywhere** except eyebrows and the hero title.

11. **Handwriting must be true.** Margin notes ("works with zero bars!", "1.47 s on
    an M3 Air", "yes, it actually emails me") restate facts from the page or code.
    Never invent a claim just because it sounds casual.
12. **The day job is two sentences.** The owner asked for "AI Engineer at Tazapay,
    Data Scientist at Microsoft before", briefly. One line of what, one line of
    impact. The projects and writing carry the page.

## Worked example: the Microsoft chapter (pass two)

This chapter no longer exists as a full page; its two numbers survive on the
Microsoft sticky note. The method still applies to any beat-style copy.

Raw material, as spoken by the owner (lightly trimmed):

> I made sure sellers could close deals really quickly using our AI agent, Seller
> Copilot. Upload the proposal documents and see what else is needed. There is a RAG
> system, a multi-agent system that sends out requests if approvals are needed. Deal
> cycle time reduced, CSAT improved, our money was stuck for a lot less time. At the
> scale of Microsoft, billions not stuck for days is a big deal. Anomaly detection
> prevented $100M+ in annual revenue leakage across $70B+ in global invoices with an
> explainable product that reframed revenue loss as a proactive product risk. We need
> not fill text-heavy details; maximise for impact.

Steps taken:

1. **Find the two outcomes.** Deal cycle (15 to 2 days, CSAT up, money unstuck) and
   leakage ($100M+ across $70B+). Everything else is supporting detail.
2. **One beat per outcome.** Four stats had "felt long"; two beats with a scene each
   is the same information with half the scroll.
3. **Title = number + result.** "Deals closed in 2 days, not 15." beats "15 → 2 days"
   because it is a sentence a person would say.
4. **Body = mechanism + who benefits + scale, in the owner's order.**
   "Seller Copilot. Upload the proposal; a RAG and multi-agent system finds what's
   missing and chases approvals itself. Sellers rated it higher, and at Microsoft's
   scale, billions stopped sitting idle for days."
5. **Second beat reframes, as the owner did.** "Revenue loss stopped being an ops clean-up
   and became a risk you see coming" is the owner's "proactive product risk rather than a
   reactive operations issue", said plainly.
6. **Check phone width.** The first body ran six lines at 375 px; one word was cut
   ("the approvals" to "approvals") and the beat box was given more height.

## Facts panel

The Who chapter's facts use a definition list with short keys (Now, Before, School,
Also, Based in, Writes at, Off hours). Each value is one line. Optional facts were
judged on one question: does this tell the reader something a job title cannot?
YC Startup School (first Bangalore edition, April 2026) passed; it says something
about ambition. A list of tools would not have.

## Things that were removed and why

- Case-study decks and a prototype: the owner asked; they read as a portfolio of
  slides, not of shipped things.
- Employment dates: removed earlier at the owner's request; the story is ordered, so dates
  add nothing.
- The Microsoft standfirst paragraph: it repeated the beats. Cut when the beats
  were rewritten.
